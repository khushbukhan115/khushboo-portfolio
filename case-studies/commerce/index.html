Selected product work · Commerce and retail

Customer-facing products, across four industries.

Across my career I have designed customer-facing digital products in loyalty, retail, consumer brands and fashion. This is the customer-facing complement to the enterprise product work in the rest of this portfolio.

How to read this page

The case studies in this portfolio walk through a problem, the decisions taken and the trade-offs accepted. This page does not, because that written record was never kept for this work, and reconstructing it now would not be honest.

What is here is the finished interface design, the business each piece was made for, and the period it was made in. There are no performance figures for this set, so none are shown or implied.

The four sit in different industries, serve different customer segments and run on different business models. One is a live transactional product where a member spends earned points and receives something. One sells drinks and merchandise directly. One asks for an appointment instead of a payment. One serves a retail buyer and a shopper in the same layout. The work was to read each business quickly and design inside its rules.

The anchor of the set · Be U · roughly 2020

Be U, a real transactional product.

Members earn points on what they spend and turn them into vouchers they actually receive. That makes this the most product-like work in the set, which is why it leads the page.

Be U is a loyalty and rewards marketplace for the Gulf, run by the retail group Beside Trading. It is not a page that describes a purchase. It is the purchase, with money and points on the line, terms that have to be read, and a delivery that has to arrive.

The design covers the whole path. A member lands on a tiered welcome, browses a category taxonomy and a partner catalog, opens a voucher and reads its validity and conditions, configures it by denomination and quantity, chooses whether it is for themselves or a gift, sees the tax position, and then receives it digitally to a wallet, by text message, by email, or as something printable.

Two constraints shaped the interface more than anything else. It had to hold in two languages. English and Arabic means the layout mirrors for right-to-left reading, so alignment, iconography and the direction of every flow had to work both ways. It had to hold on both desktop and mobile. The same tier welcome, the same reward list and the same checkout, at both sizes, without a second design. That pushed the work towards reusable pieces: one card, one category tile, one offer row, one voucher template, repeated across a catalog that keeps taking on new partners.

An honest note on this system

Yellow on white at small sizes, and the thin display face used for headings, are the contrast risks in this design. They are worth naming rather than hiding. On a system this transactional, that is the thing I would revisit first today.

A consumer brand, sold directly · Faygo · mid 2010s

A loud brand, held together by one device.

Faygo is a Detroit soft-drink brand, founded in 1907 and part of the culture of the American Midwest. Its site had to do three jobs at once.

Carry a brand voice that is genuinely loud. Help people find the flavours in shops near them. And sell drinks and merchandise directly to the people who cannot find them.

What holds it together is a single diagonal colour-block device, used as a system rather than a decoration. It becomes the navigation mosaic, the overlay on photography, and the frame for a recipe card, so a palette that would otherwise be chaos gets structure. Oversized display type sets the hierarchy, and the brand's own voice runs through the small copy. Storefront, product page and editorial all read as one thing, at several widths.

A considered purchase · Vintage Galleria · roughly 2015

Restraint, for a purchase people think about.

Nobody buys a hand-finished interior piece out of a cart, so this site is not built around one.

Vintage Galleria is a Karachi gallery of bespoke and handcrafted interiors and decor. Its business is consultative. The site has to convey the quality of the work, show the collection well enough to be judged, and get someone to book an appointment or send an enquiry.

So the layout is deliberately quiet, and it is the opposite register to Faygo. A full-bleed landing, a persistent left navigation with a clear active state, a lot of negative space, and a browsing grid where the captions appear on hover instead of competing with the pieces. Hierarchy comes from scale and space rather than ornament, with one gold accent and a script wordmark carrying the signal. Some captions in these screens are still placeholder text, because this is a design-stage capture rather than the final populated site.

Two audiences, one layout · Marble · roughly 2014

One site, a trade buyer and a shopper.

Marble is a Glasgow womenswear house whose knitwear co-ordinates sell through several hundred stockists across the United Kingdom, Europe and Canada.

That split its audience in a way most fashion sites do not have to handle. A retail buyer needs to judge whether the range is worth stocking, how deliveries work, and how well it sells through. A shopper wants to see the look and find somewhere to buy it. Both arrive at the same address.

The site is mobile-first and responsive, on a dark theme with large type and a navigation bar pinned at the foot. The part worth pointing at is Why Marble: a numbered argument written for a buyer, about sizing, capsule ranges, delivery scheduling, point of sale support and sell-through, sitting inside the same layout a consumer browses. One design serves both, with no separate trade portal to build and maintain.

What the set adds up to

Capabilities across the customer lifecycle.

Read together rather than brand by brand, this work covers every stage a customer moves through in a transactional product, and every stage a business needs someone to own.

01

Discover

Catalogs, taxonomies and browsing. Be U's category structure and partner catalog, Faygo's flavour and shop navigation, Vintage Galleria's browsing grid.

02

Compare

Detail pages and terms that let someone choose with confidence. Be U's voucher detail with validity and conditions, Faygo's product page with variants and a cross-sell row.

03

Configure

Options and pricing before payment. Be U's denomination chips, quantity cap, stated tax position, and the branch between buying for yourself and buying as a gift.

04

Purchase

The transactional path itself, held on desktop and on mobile, and in English and in Arabic with the layout mirrored for right-to-left reading.

05

Return

What happens after payment. Be U's issued voucher, delivered to a wallet, sent to a mobile number, sent to an email address, or printed, with its number and terms attached.

06

Re-engage

Reasons to come back. Be U's Gold and Black tiers and rotating partner offers, Faygo's editorial and recipes, Marble's seasonal collections and event diary.

Provenance and periods

Be U, Faygo, Vintage Galleria and Marble are real businesses and these are screens of real work. Each period is stated on its section and in the ledger above, so nothing here should be read as current. The older pieces are shown because the discipline in them still holds, not because the styling does.

Marble's site of this era was produced with the Glasgow agency Refractiv, and that credit is visible in the footer of those screens. No performance figures are claimed anywhere on this page, because none exist for this work.
