## 1 · Portfolio blueprint — section order

A nine-section vertical scroll, alternating dark and cream bands for rhythm. Each section answers exactly one question, so the visuals carry the story and the reader can scan it in under three minutes.

1. **Hero** (dark) — *What was this, and is it real?* The reframe (access, not capability) + honest scope chips + the live product.
2. **The Opportunity** (cream) — *Why did the market need it?* SME value of gift cards; the barrier was infrastructure, not demand.
3. **Why Merit could build it** (dark) — *Why was this a smart bet?* Merit already owned the hard part; the product exposed a thin, safe slice.
4. **The Design Thesis** (cream) — *What was the core idea?* Subtraction, not shrinking. **Signature waterline diagram.**
5. **The Experience** (dark) — *What did you actually design?* Onboarding → workspace → card creator → storefront → recipient → lifecycle. The longest, most visual section.
6. **Hidden Complexity** (cream) — *What was hard?* The service ecosystem, occasion-led merchandising, and the three tensions.
7. **The Value it created** (dark) — *Who did it pay off for?* A three-sided value model, honestly tagged.
8. **Timeline & Attribution** (cream) — *What was yours vs. the company’s?* Three ownership bands kept deliberately separate.
9. **Reflection** (dark) — *What did you learn?* Productisation as a subtraction problem; instrument next time.

**Why not the diagnosis-with-metrics arc?** There are no product analytics or user research on record. Forcing a funnel-metrics structure would require inventing evidence. The arc instead rests on the one thing the evidence fully supports — design reasoning about what to expose and what to hide.

## 5 · Annotation copy (design-review notes on the key screens)

**Onboarding (progressive disclosure).** “Every field is optional except email. The account opens on the minimum viable identity; TRN/VAT, billing and card details surface only when the merchant is ready to transact — so the first screen never feels like an enterprise setup form.”

**Card creator — why this screen exists.** “It is the one place the subtraction thesis has to hold: a non-specialist builds a sellable, branded card without meeting issuance, payments, or redemption.”

**Card creator — interaction decision.** “Left rail = a short, guided task list (Logo → Background → Text). Right = a live preview that resolves ‘what will my customer actually receive?’ in real time.”

**Card creator — trust & scale.** “‘Powered by GiftCardsby’ and a structured card object stay fixed, so every merchant card is issuable and trackable no matter how freely they style it.”

**Storefront / recipient — trust signals held visible.** “Value, validity (‘1 year from date of issue’), the merchant’s identity and terms stay legible right through purchase and into the recipient’s inbox — the terms of the deal are never hidden.”

**Storefront / recipient — one promise, three people.** “The buyer configures, the recipient receives, the merchant redeems. A shared, previewable card object keeps all three parties confident they are acting on the same thing.”

**Occasion merchandising — freedom on the surface, structure underneath.** “Merchants get real freedom for multicultural occasions — Ramadan and Eid, Christmas, National Day, weddings, corporate thank-yous — while the platform keeps the structural fields it needs to issue, price and track every card. New occasion designs are cheap to add.”

- **One real, directional outcome metric** — the single highest-value addition. The case is honest about not having adoption/revenue data; any true launch-window number would materially de-risk the “metrics vacuum” objection.

## Provenance & honesty guardrails (kept intact)

- **No GiftCardsby performance metrics** are claimed. The only numbers shown are the industry-cited gift-card behaviour stat (Public · Gulf Business, Aug 2022) and dated launch facts.
- **Merit’s network figures** (5,000 brands, 20M users, etc.) are deliberately **absent** — they are the company’s enabling asset, never presented as this product’s result.
- **No user research is claimed.** Framing is domain/product reasoning.
- **Role attribution** is disciplined: experience/interface design is first-person; productisation strategy, commercial model, talabat, and the platform are attributed to Merit’s teams.
- Provenance chips used: **Public** (blue), **Qualitative** (yellow), **Inference** (purple), **Context** (outline).

## Build notes

- **Brand:** re-themed from the earlier orange editorial accent to the **true product palette — blue (#3E7BD0) + yellow (#F4C81E)** on a near-black editorial base, with cream bands. More truthful to the live product and more premium.
- **Type:** Space Grotesk (display), Inter (body), IBM Plex Mono (eyebrows/labels) — all present in the file.
- **Structure:** one master frame, nine auto-layout section frames, 1180-wide content column, fully responsive to width. Everything is editable.
- Source of truth: `case-study-content-master.md`, `case-study-knowledge-base.md`. Narrative parity with the existing `index.html`; this is the visual-first Figma expression of it.
