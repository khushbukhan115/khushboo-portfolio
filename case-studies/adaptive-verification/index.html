Case study · KYC Verification

Faster identity verification. Fewer steps, fewer drop-offs.

A global identity verification provider runs the KYC checks that other businesses rely on to confirm a new user is who they claim to be. I led the redesign of its document verification flow, shifting work off the user and onto the system so the check confirms what it can automatically and asks the person only when it needs to.

The problem

A check that failed valid users.

The verification worked, but it front-loaded manual steps. It asked people to classify their own document before the camera opened, then captured the front and back of a single ID as two separate sessions.

Two behaviours recurred in production. People selected one document type and then presented a different but valid one, and the mismatch caused a decline. And because a mid-capture confirmation looked like the end of the process, many stopped early or re-shot the wrong side. Both behaviours were predictable, given what the flow asked people to do.

The insight, and the move

Ask only for what the system cannot work out.

The screens weren't the real problem. We were asking users to supply information the system could already determine from the document itself.

The redesign moved that work to the system: it reads the country and document type from the capture, checks image quality automatically, and captures front and back in one continuous step. The rule behind each decision was consistent, ask the user only for what the system cannot reliably determine, with a fallback for the cases where it can't.

I did not ship this as a single redesign. My initial plan was to remove the document-selection screen outright, but the early evidence showed the product wasn't ready for that, so I changed the approach. Each change shipped as a small, production-validated step, released to lower-risk customers first and measured before the next, until there was enough evidence to safely remove a screen the wider architecture depended on.

What is measured and what is modelled
The two rates above are observed, taken from production analytics across two matched 15-day windows on comparable traffic. The estimate of additional legitimate users who now complete onboarding is modelled from those rates, not a measured KPI, and is labelled as such in the detailed case study. Verification integrity held: the flow got shorter without loosening the check.
The most demanding review came from the company's most risk-averse enterprise customer, who adopted the redesign only after it had been proven in production.
